Can we feed? Which route?reference

Any of these = NPO

  • Severe birth asphyxia: NPO 4–6 days
  • Respiratory distress, RR > 80/min
  • BW < 1500 g: observe RR and apnea for 24 h first

If no contraindication, how to feed (early feeding is the new trend)

  • GA < 32 wk, BW < 1500 g, RR 60–80/min: tube feeding (if RR > 80/min: NPO + IV)
  • GA > 34 wk, BW > 1500 g, RR < 60/min: breastfeeding if no contraindication, or cup feeding

Contraindications to breastfeeding

  • Maternal HIV
  • HTLV or CMV infection
  • Narcotic / drug addiction
  • Chemotherapy or I-131
  • Active TB, treated under 2 weeks
  • Herpes at the breast

How often

  • Term: 6 feeds/day (every 3–4 h). Preterm: 8 feeds/day (every 2–3 h). The calculator starts at 8, as in the sheet's worked examples.
  • If the mother breastfeeds: as much as the baby wants, not over 30 min per feed, until the breast is emptied so the baby gets the fatty hind milk. Schedule every 3–4 h and wake the baby to feed if needed.

Calculation aid only. Based on the Newborn Feeding calculation sheet and the PCMC maintenance table; check against your unit protocol and the baby's clinical state. TV = volume ÷ weight (mL/kg/day); TC = TV × kcal per oz ÷ 30 (kcal/kg/day). BM = expressed breast milk, IF = infant formula 20 kcal/oz, HMF/PF = human milk fortifier / preterm formula 24 kcal/oz.
